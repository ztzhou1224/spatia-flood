# What we know about measuring first-floor height, and the combined benchmark (2026-10-06)

Summary of the research of 2026-10-05/06 on branch `claude/rules-in-force`. Every number is from a committed output
file named next to it; details and caveats are in the linked notes. Answer keys are used for scoring only.

## 0. Review corrections (2026-10-06)

An independent review (Fable 5.1) found no answer-key leakage and confirmed the benchmark numbers, and found four bugs
that changed reported numbers: (1) the conformal bands used an interpolated quantile that under-covers on small
calibration sets; (2) the "new area" bands were calibrated on within-area residuals; (3) the no-label baselines of
the local-sample tests were scored on a different set of houses; (4) "flagged-only" samples in B were partly random.
All four are fixed and rerun; this summary, LPC.md and BANDS.md carry the corrected numbers, and the weight / sample
design was re-chosen in a pre-registered test (chosen on two areas, confirmed on the third). Withdrawn claims: "50
local houses restore 0.84-0.85 band coverage with 90-92% of decided calls right" (bands are honest but much wider);
"borrowed bands fail only for raised houses"; "flagged-only labels lower raised recall in Cypress Creek"; "eave
minus stories errs more on two-story houses" (Meyerland reverses it); "point density is not the limit" (only roof /
eave tested).

## 1. The question changed: measure the house, do not predict it

The first-floor height of an existing house is a fact, not a forecast. The experiments show why a model trained on
other houses hits a ceiling: inputs that describe the type of house and place (foundation mix, age, flood zone, soil,
climate, the flood rule in force) never observe the house, so a model from elsewhere stays at about 2 ft error, and
30 locally measured houses beat every input-only similarity measure (`similarity/SIMILARITY.md`). What helps is
evidence about the specific building: lidar ground and roof, appraisal records, images.

## 3. Combined benchmark (`harris_mini/benchmark.py`, output `harris_mini/benchmark_output.txt`)

Each method adds one kind of evidence; F and G use the label-free physical estimate. Answer-key screen applied
(3 of 3,332 Meyerland, 8 of 5,127 Cypress Creek and 19 of 2,539 Clear Lake values fail it). "BFE side" = share of SFHA houses whose
estimated floor is on the correct side of the BFE.

"National" in methods A and B means national inputs (DEM ground, NSI); method B is still TRAINED on Harris labels. No
truly national model with these features has been tested (review, 2026-10-06).

**Clear Lake (C), 5 folds by 1 km block** (198 raised houses, 589 in the SFHA with a BFE)

| method | MAE ft (95% CI) | within 1 ft | raised MAE | raised precision / recall | BFE side |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A national default (DEM ground + NSI height) | 0.98 (0.64-1.37) | 79% | 6.78 | - | 80.5% |
| B national model | 0.68 (0.45-0.95) | 89% | 4.70 | 0.57 / 0.57 | 83.4% |
| C + point cloud | 0.56 (0.41-0.75) | 92% | 3.29 | 0.80 / 0.69 | 86.6% |
| D + records | 0.56 | 92% | 3.42 | 0.81 / 0.70 | 85.2% |
| E + eave estimate | **0.55** (0.39-0.75) | 92% | **3.27** | 0.78 / 0.69 | 86.1% |
| F E with physical override for flagged houses | 0.62 | 91% | 3.51 | 0.77 / **0.73** | **87.3%** |
| G physical estimate only | 1.81 | 66% | 4.08 | 0.35 / 0.75 | 81.5% |

**Cypress Creek (B), 5 folds by 1 km block** (49 raised, 111 in the SFHA): the national model is already at
0.29 ft MAE and 94.6% BFE side; point cloud and records change MAE by < 0.01 ft (BFE side up to 96.4% with records);
raised recall stays low (0.08-0.14) with only 49 raised houses to learn from.

**Meyerland (A), 5 folds by 1 km block** (third area, added 2026-10-06; 599 raised, mostly 3-5 ft; 2,995 in the
SFHA with a BFE): national default 1.02 ft MAE, BFE side 69.9%; national model 0.49 ft, 86.8%; E **0.46** ft
(0.40-0.53), raised MAE 1.11 ft, precision / recall 0.87 / 0.76, BFE side 86.9%; F 0.65 ft, raised 1.94 ft,
BFE side 87.7%; G 1.70 ft.

**New area (model trained on the other two areas pooled)**, screened:

|---|---|---|---|
| A Meyerland | 0.80 / 0.04 / 71.9% | 0.70 / 2.12 / 0.28 / 78.3% | 0.81 / 2.44 / **0.55** / **82.3%** |

A model trained elsewhere misses most raised houses where they are common (A recall 0.04 for the national model). The
physical override F recovers them in both raised areas (recall 0.28 -> 0.55 in A, 0.55 -> 0.68 in C) and puts more
houses on the right side of the BFE, but in Meyerland, whose raised houses are modest, it worsens MAE (0.70 -> 0.81
ft): the eave estimate is too coarse (G alone 1.70 ft) for 3-5 ft lifts. In Cypress Creek the extra evidence lowers
MAE but costs BFE side (94.6% -> 87.4% on 111 SFHA houses). (The first, two-area version gave B -> C: E 0.75, F 0.71
ft; C -> B: 0.50 -> 0.54 ft; replaced by this table.)

**Images, own model (Qwen3-VL-4B)** on the ~30 houses with a usable view: Bee Maps (21 houses, none raised) 1 false
raised call, stories agree 94%; Mapillary (15 houses, 4 raised) raised precision / recall 0.50 / 0.50, stories 58%.

## 4. What the benchmark says

1. **Lidar is the main evidence.** Ground from the DEM is the first step (A); the point cloud is the second
   (Clear Lake MAE 0.68 -> 0.56 ft, raised error 4.7 -> 3.3 ft, BFE side 83 -> 87%).
2. **Where raised houses are rare, a local model already does well** (Cypress Creek 0.29 ft); the evidence layers
   matter where raised houses are common.
3. **For a new area, the label-free physical estimate finds the raised houses, but is coarse.** A model trained
   elsewhere misses raised houses; overriding it with eave-minus-stories on flagged houses doubles raised recall in
   Meyerland and cuts raised error by 28% in Clear Lake, but adds error where lifts are small (Meyerland MAE 0.70 ->
   0.81 ft). Bands and a small local calibration sample matter more than the point estimate there (below).
4. **Records and images are flags, not measurements.** Records add little once the point cloud is in; images
   cannot yet be scored on raised houses for lack of coverage.
5. **Remaining error is concentrated** in a few neighbourhoods of raised, often attached or multi-unit buildings:
   the 95% intervals on raised error are wide (about 2-4 ft).

**Error bands (`harris_mini/bands.py`, `harris_mini/BANDS.md`), three areas, corrected after review.** Within an
area, 90% conformal bands cover 0.89-0.91 of houses. Borrowed from the other two areas (pool from cross-area
pseudo-experiments) they cover 0.84-0.99: roughly right for ordinary houses (0.86-0.99), wrong for raised ones in an
area-dependent way (A flagged bands 13.7 ft wide; C flagged coverage 0.66 with 63% of decided calls right).
Calibrated on 30 unflagged + 20 flagged local houses with the finite-sample quantile they are honest (0.93-0.94;
flagged 0.90-1.00) but wide (unflagged median 4.6 / 1.9 / 6.4 ft in A / B / C), so the BFE side is decided for only
11% / 58% / 31% of SFHA houses (89-100% of those calls right). Narrow honest bands need more local labels per group
(100 random: unflagged 2.9 / 1.4 / 2.1 ft).

**2018 vs 2024 lidar change (`harris_mini/lpc_change.py`, `eval_change.py`, LPC.md).** Heights above each flight's own
ground (datum and subsidence cancel). Meyerland: 237 of 6,750 houses rose > 3 ft, mostly teardowns rebuilt as
elevated two-story houses (HCAD 2025: 452 rebuilt since 2018, 383 from one to two stories; eave +17 ft); 43 lifts in
place (+5.5-6 ft). Clear Lake: 38 rose, 36 of them lifts in place of about 10 ft, 15 confirmed by a slab -> piers
record change. A ridge-free rule catches 396 of 452 rebuilds (trees cleared for construction hide them from the
ridge). The answer key predates most of these changes (Feb 2018 captures), so changed houses carry 2-3.5x the error,
but they are 1.4-3% of houses: not the main error source. Change detection cannot see houses raised before 2018.

## 5. Recommended pipeline for the Harris pilot (own models only)

1. DEM ground ring (lowest adjacent grade) for every house.
2. Point-cloud roof / eave per footprint; HCAD records (stories, lower level, basement).
3. Model trained on local labels where they exist (certificates, the HCFCD key); elsewhere the national model.
4. Flag raised houses (model > 3 ft, record lower level / basement, eave estimate > 3 ft) and, for flagged houses
   in a new area, use the eave-minus-stories height.
5. Images only for flagged houses and only for categories (raised, piers vs enclosure, stories), read by a
   self-hosted Qwen3-VL on rectified views; height stays geometric.
6. Every value carries its evidence (measured / observed / inferred), dates and an error band. In a new area,
   measure about 50 houses (30 unflagged + 20 from the raised-flag list) and refit the model with them (weight 10;
   chosen on two areas, confirmed on the third); this beats the physical override. Bands for ordinary houses can be
   borrowed until local labels exist; flagged houses are "too close to call" on the BFE until local flagged labels
   calibrate them; 50 labels give honest but wide bands, narrow ones need 100+. Check the record flag's precision
   in each area (0.08-0.89 within Harris).
7. Compare the newest lidar flight with the previous one (single-return roofs, no ridge) to flag every house lifted
   or rebuilt since, and re-estimate those from the newest flight (records give the year built: lift vs rebuild).
   For unchanged houses the newer flight is no more accurate.
