Screening, not a determination. Not for insurance rating, permitting or a purchase decision without the building's Elevation Certificate.

What this map can and cannot tell you

Release pinellas-r1 (Pinellas County, FL). From the internal review of the first release (spatia-flood docs/07) and what the second one changed (docs/09).

1. Modeled floors are least reliable on elevated houses

Most buildings have no Elevation Certificate, so their floor is estimated from lidar. Against state (FDEM) certificates the model has not seen, the estimate is off by about 1 ft on average; against the county's own certificates in the same held-out areas, which are mostly newer, elevated houses, by about 2 ft. For an elevated house the model does not mark as raised, the shown range contains the real floor only about three times in four (few such houses were available to measure this), and the misses are mostly floors higher than shown. A "below the BFE" on such a house is the call most likely to be wrong. Both scores are on the county card.

2. Ground, certificates and new buildings

  • Ground. "Ground" is the middle (median) lidar height in a narrow ring around the footprint, with water and seawall toes removed. On sloped or terraced lots, or next to a wall or the water, it is less certain; the record says "ground suspect" there.
  • Certificates. Only certificates of finished construction are used as records. Certificates from construction drawings or taken during construction are listed on the record but not used.
  • Buildings newer than the lidar. The lidar was flown in winter 2018-19. A building the property records date to 2019 or later gets no modeled floor ("stale"); its roof and ground may describe the lot before it.

3. "Above" or "below" compares the first living floor

The main call compares the first floor people live on with the BFE. For an elevated house with an enclosed area underneath (garage, storage), the NFIP "lowest floor" is usually that enclosure, which can be below the BFE even when the living floor is above it. Where a certificate gives it, the record also shows the lowest floor and its own call; for modeled houses the lowest floor is not known. AO zones (shallow flooding given as a depth) get no BFE and no call. Where a certificate disagrees with the lidar by many feet, no call is made.

4. What a local reviewer will check first is not in the map yet

  • FEMA issued a preliminary countywide FIRM for Pinellas on 2025-05-15; the map uses the effective FIRM (each record names the panel date and the county's FIRM status).
  • Letters of Map Amendment (LOMAs) that take a building out of the floodplain are not applied.
  • Between FEMA's BFE lines, a BFE is estimated only where the path between the two lines stays mostly inside the building's own flood zone; elsewhere there is no BFE and no call.

Units and datum

Elevations are feet NAVD88 (US survey feet). Lidar values use the GEOID12B geoid; certificates do not state their geoid. Heights (floor, roof, eave) are feet above the lidar ground described above.
