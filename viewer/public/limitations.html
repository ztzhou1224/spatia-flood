Screening, not a determination. Not for insurance rating, permitting or a purchase decision without the building's Elevation Certificate.

What this map can and cannot tell you

Release pinellas-r0 (Pinellas County, FL). From the internal review of this release (spatia-flood docs/07); each item is fixed in the next release (docs/09).

1. Modeled floors are less accurate on elevated houses than the headline number

Most buildings have no Elevation Certificate, so their floor is estimated from lidar. Scored against certificates from the state (FDEM) the estimate is off by about 1 ft on average; scored against the county's own certificates, which the model never saw and which are mostly newer, elevated houses, it is off by about 2.2 ft. For an elevated house that the model does not mark as raised, the shown range contains the real floor only a bit more than half the time, and the misses are mostly floors that are higher than shown. A "below the BFE" on such a house is the call most likely to be wrong. Both scores are on the county card, each with the houses it was measured on.

2. Some values are not what their label says

  • Ground. "Ground" is the lowest lidar point in a narrow ring around the footprint. Next to a canal or a seawall that is the water or the seawall base, not the yard; on sloped lots it is the low side. On those lots the floor height above ground looks too high, and the building may be marked "raised" when it is not.
  • Certificates that are designs. Some certificates were filed from construction drawings or during construction. They are shown as records but describe a planned floor, not a measured one.
  • Buildings newer than the lidar. The lidar was flown in winter 2018-19. A building built later is estimated from the ground as it was before construction, even though its date says 2018-19.

3. "Above" or "below" compares the first living floor

The call compares the first floor people live on with the BFE. For an elevated house with an enclosed area underneath (garage, storage), the NFIP "lowest floor" is that enclosure, which is usually below the BFE even when the living floor is above it. For AO zones (shallow flooding with a depth, not an elevation) the call uses a neighbouring zone's BFE and should be ignored. Some "above" calls rest on certificates that disagree with the lidar by many feet; those are marked on the building's record.

4. What a local reviewer will check first is not in the map yet

  • FEMA issued a preliminary countywide FIRM for Pinellas on 2025-05-15; the map uses the effective FIRM.
  • Letters of Map Amendment (LOMAs) that take a building out of the floodplain are not applied.
  • The county has three FIRM effective dates; each building shows the date of the panel it sits on.

Units and datum

Elevations are feet NAVD88 (US survey feet). Lidar values use the GEOID12B geoid; certificates do not state their geoid. Heights (floor, roof, eave) are feet above the lidar ground described above.
